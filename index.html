My Role & Objectives

During my 6-month practicum, my primary objective was to handle data transformation, streamline reporting workflows, and ultimately eliminate manual calculation errors using advanced Excel functions and VBA macros.

Week 1: 6th April to 10th April

Started my journey by officially reporting to the Hire Purchase Supervision Division (HPSD), specifically under the Reporting & AKPK and FIS & Administration (SSTF) unit, right after signing all my documents with HR. I spent some time reading through the HR manual just to understand the core rules of surviving in the bank. Luckily, there was another intern named Howard in the same subunit who was in his final week. I basically stuck to him like glue—having lunch, working together, and trying to absorb all his knowledge to get a head start so I wouldn't have to bother the seniors too much once he left. We ended the week with a farewell gathering for him alongside seniors from the other subunits.

Week 2 & 3: 13th April to 24th April

Reality hit as I started working on my first monthly report, the Loan in Arrears report. I had to work with a massive chunk of raw data extracted from SAS programming into Excel. From there, it was an endless cycle of data cleaning and preprocessing, dealing with a crazy amount of rounding and balancing between PBB and PIBB for HP and IL accounts. The following week, I moved on to a new beast called the Daily Recovery report. It involved more data cleaning, heavy rounding, and another massive dose of VLOOKUPs to transfer everything into a ready format. Watching the recovery progress slowly accumulate day by day was oddly satisfying, though.

Week 4: 27th April to 1st May

Decided to take things into my own hands and started exploring Excel VBA macros on my own just to see what kind of magic they could do. For my first ever macro project, I set out to automate the tedious data transfer process for the Daily Recovery report. I sketched out a rough idea of the logic, ran into a few annoying failures during testing, but eventually got the code to successfully transfer the cleaned data. Feeling confident, I enhanced the code to automate three other related files. I even added a prompt that automatically switched the live VLOOKUP formulas into static values, letting us send the report around without any restraints.

Week 5 to 9: 4th May to 5th June

This month was all about leveling up my automation skills. I tackled the Make Model report, organizing HP loan data based on vehicle categories, and then immediately built my second macro to handle its complex 3D balancing rounding process (horizontal and vertical loops until the checking difference was exactly zero). Jumped straight into my third and fourth macro projects for the Loan in Arrears report, writing code to dynamically do extensive rounding adjustments and automatically VLOOKUP desired data into templates based on filename prefixes. Ended the month handling the Overall Delinquency report, making it easier for the team to do trend analysis.

Week 10 to 12: 8th June to 26th June

The Data Cleansing Era

Spent these weeks solving major database extraction bugs for the IT division. First, I built a "Phone Number Formatter" macro to detect and fix phone numbers where the starting '0' was cut off by adding '60' or '65' prefixes. Then, I developed a "Hirer/Guarantor Separator" to filter out scattered duplicate data across three different legacy systems, saving the bank serious money on SMS blasting costs. Finally, I built a post-separation formatter to fix a bug where 12-digit phone numbers were spilling over and corrupting the email column in the database.

Week 13: 29th June to 3rd July

Worked on a new weekly workflow macro called the "Multi-Category Master to Target Sorter" which processed five different ageing categories and separated them into target files for eight different Credit Administration Centres. In the middle of all this, I bumped into a massive PBB fire drill. The alarm went off in the evening, and we had to follow procedure, slowly climbing down 22 flights of stairs to head to the open parking lot.

Week 14 to 17: 6th July to 31st July

Graduated to handling high-value accounts. I took on the HP_1mil and HP/FSF_3mil reports, zooming in on accounts with outstanding balances above 1 million and 3 million respectively. I tracked their Behavioral scores (B-scores) and days in arrears to flag high-risk accounts. I also built the "Base Listing Multi-Category Sorter" macro to track how many accounts transitioned into newly Close Attention (CA) or Impaired Loan (IL) status. Ended July managing the Restructure & Reschedule (R&R) report, recalculating delinquency ratios and tracking mandatory nursing periods.

Week 18: 3rd August to 7th August

Assisted with the data transformation for Expected Credit Loss (ECL) data. I analyzed accounts that either improved or worsened, transferred the cleaned data into the report template, and submitted it to my senior for checking before sending it to our external auditor EY for justification. The absolute highlight of the week was joining the massive 60th anniversary Public Bank event at the HQ—enjoying the food, fireworks, and a drone show.

Week 19 to 23: 10th August to 11th September

A heavy analytical phase. Helped compile the draft 2026 yearly report of Hire Purchase for BNM, ensuring all strict requirements were met. Coded a brand new interactive project called "IL Ratio Sensitivity & Scenario Analysis," applying a Userform in Excel to forecast future IL ratios and help managers plan strategies. Handled the CA Breakdown, Margin Earnterm, and Luxury Car reports. By week 23, I brought out the advanced Excel functions to completely optimize workflows—using `UNIQUE`, `LET`, and Power Query to process data into one massive unified table.

Week 24: 14th September to 18th September

Went back into all the macro projects I had built over the internship to do some final housekeeping. I enhanced them so they would run smoothly and avoid crashing during runtime. I spent time showing my seniors how to run each macro in the correct order, setting them all up in a single folder with unique names so it would be super convenient for the team to use after I left. Ended the week having an early farewell dinner with the seniors from the other subunits.

Week 25 & 26: 21st September to 30th September

The final stretch! We welcomed a new intern who was also joining for six months. I guided her through the tasks I had been working on, showed her around the department, and explained all the necessary Hire Purchase knowledge so she could understand how the whole team functions. I had a great farewell lunch with the team and received some awesome surprises, gifts, and cakes on my last day of work. After an amazing six months, I finally signed off as an intern.
